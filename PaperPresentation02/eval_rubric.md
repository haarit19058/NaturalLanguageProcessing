# CS613 Paper Presentation — Guidelines & Evaluation Rubric

**Total: 15 marks per student**

The presentation is conducted in groups. While the paper and presentation are prepared collectively, **each student is assessed individually** based on:

- their understanding of the paper,
- their contribution, and
- their ability to explain and defend the work.

> The primary objective is **not** to create a flashy slide deck. The objective is to demonstrate a genuine understanding of the paper and communicate that understanding effectively to the class.

---

## 1. What should the presentation cover?

The presentation should help the audience answer the following questions.

### A. What problem does the paper solve?
- What is the problem?
- Why is it important?
- What were the limitations of existing approaches?
- What motivated the authors?

### B. What is the key contribution?
- What is the central idea?
- What is novel?
- What is the main insight of the paper?
- Why does the proposed approach work?

### C. How does the method work?
Explain the important technical components, such as:
- Architecture
- Algorithms
- Objective / loss functions
- Training procedure
- Important design decisions
- Equations and mathematical formulation

> **Do not simply reproduce equations or figures from the paper. Explain them.**

### D. What are the experimental findings?
Discuss:
- Experimental setup
- Datasets
- Baselines
- Evaluation metrics
- Main results
- Important ablations

> Focus on the **most important findings**, rather than reproducing every table in the paper.

### E. What are the limitations?
Discuss critically:
- What are the strengths of the work?
- What are its limitations?
- Are the experiments sufficient?
- What assumptions does the method make?
- What could be improved?

### F. What is the impact of the paper?
Briefly discuss how the paper influenced subsequent research or modern NLP/LLM systems.

---

## 2. Team Contribution Slide

Every group must include **one dedicated slide titled "Team Contribution"**, presented **at the end** of the presentation, clearly indicating the contribution of every group member.

For each member, report whether the contribution was **Equal** or **Partial**, and assign a contribution score.

**Example:**

| Student   | Contribution | Score |
|-----------|--------------|-------|
| Student A | Equal        | 1.0   |
| Student B | Equal        | 1.0   |
| Student C | Partial      | 0.8   |
| Student D | Partial      | 0.6   |

The contribution scores should reflect the actual relative contribution to the group work, including preparation, understanding, slide development, experiments/analysis where applicable, and presentation preparation.

### Important
- The contribution score is **not** the final individual mark. It is one input into the individual assessment.
- Be honest and fair. Inflating everyone's score to 1.0 when contributions were clearly unequal may be considered when evaluating the group.

If all members genuinely contributed equally, you may simply report:

| Student   | Contribution | Score |
|-----------|--------------|-------|
| Student A | Equal        | 1.0   |
| Student B | Equal        | 1.0   |
| Student C | Equal        | 1.0   |
| Student D | Equal        | 1.0   |

---

## 3. Evaluation — 15 Marks

| Criterion               | Marks | Description                                                                          |
|-------------------------|:-----:|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Problem & Motivation    | 2     | Understanding of the problem and motivation                                          |
| Core Contribution       | 3     | Understanding and explanation of the central idea                                    |
| Technical Understanding | 4     | Understanding of methodology, architecture, equations, algorithms and design choices |
| Experiments & Results   | 2     | Understanding and interpretation of experiments and results                          |
| Critical Analysis       | 2     | Strengths, limitations, assumptions and insights                                     |
| Communication & Q&A     | 2     | Clarity of explanation and ability to answer questions                               |
| **Total**               | **15**|                                                                                      |

The above criteria assess the **individual student's** understanding, even though the presentation is prepared as a group.

---

## 4. Individual Assessment

A student may receive a different mark from their teammates. For example, two students in the same presentation may receive:

- Student A — 14/15
- Student B — 11/15
- Student C — 8/15

depending on their individual understanding, contribution, and performance during Q&A.

The instructor may ask **different questions to different group members** to assess individual understanding.

---

## 5. Presentation Quality ≠ Slide Design

You will **not** receive additional marks for:
- Fancy slide templates
- Animations
- Excessive graphics
- Complex visual effects
- Large numbers of slides
- Reproducing figures from the paper

A simple slide deck with excellent technical explanations can receive full marks. In contrast, a visually impressive presentation with shallow understanding will receive a low score.

> **A useful rule:** If you put something on a slide, you should be able to explain why it is there.

---

## 6. What makes an excellent presentation?

An excellent presentation should make the audience feel:

> *"I now understand why this paper was written, what the key idea is, how it works, why it works, and what its limitations are."*

The best presentations will **teach** the paper, rather than simply summarize the paper.

### Presentation Format
- **Recommended:** 15-minute presentation + 10 minutes Q&A.
- The Q&A is an important part of the evaluation. Students should be prepared to answer questions about **any part of the paper**, not only the slides they personally presented.

### Possible Q&A questions
- Why did the authors choose this architecture?
- Why this loss function?
- What happens if this component is removed?
- Why is this baseline appropriate?
- What does this equation actually mean?
- What does this result demonstrate?
- Is the experimental evidence sufficient for the authors' claim?
- What is the biggest limitation of the paper?
- How would you improve the approach?
- How does this work relate to modern LLMs?

> The ability to answer such questions is one of the strongest indicators that you have actually read and understood the paper.
